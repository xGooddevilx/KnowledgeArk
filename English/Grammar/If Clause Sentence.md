### If Clause :

**If clauses** (also called _conditional sentences_) express a condition and its result. They have two parts:

- **If-clause** (the condition) → _If it rains..._
- **Main clause** (the result) → _...I will stay home._

#### Zero conditional

Used for facts, scientific truths, and habits — things that are ALWAYS true. Both parts are in simple present.

> Formula : simple present + simple present

- You can replace "If" with "When" and the meaning stays the same: "When you heat water, it boils."

e.g: If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.

#### First conditional

Used for real, likely situations in the future. The condition is possible.

> Formula : simple present + future term (will + base verb)

- Instead of "will", you can also use: can, may, might, should, must

e.g. If it rains, you should take an umbrella.

#### Second conditional

Used for imaginary or unlikely situations NOW or in the future. The speaker knows this is not real.

> Formula : simple past, would + base verb

e.g: If she knew the answer, she would tell us.

#### Third conditional

Used for imagining a different past. Something did NOT happen — we are imagining what would have been different. Often expresses regret.

> Formula : past perfect + would + have + past participle

e.g: If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

#### Mixed conditional :
when the time of the if-clause and the time of the result are different — one is about the past and the other is about the present (or future)
##### Type 1 : Past condition + Present result
The condition is in the PAST, but the result is happening NOW. 

> Formula : past perfect + would + base verb

e.g : If I have continued the english class, I would be a native speaker now.

* Key word to spot: now / right now / today / currently in the result clause — these signal that the outcome is about the present moment, even though the cause was in the past.


#### Type 2 : Present condition + Past result
he condition is about NOW (who you are), but the result is about the PAST.

> Formula : simple past (were / have) + past participle ( would have + p.p)

e.g: If I were braver, I would have spoken to her yesterday.
e.g : If she had listened, she would'nt has got fired yesterday.
